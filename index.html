Our Story

A small kitchen on the east side of Indy

A bowl of phở is a quiet thing. Slow water, patient bones, hours of waiting. This is the story of one such bowl — and a family who decided that East 82nd Street was as good a place as any to keep it alive.

— Chapter I —

Nước Lèo

The Broth

Before the lunch crowd, before the lights come on out front, the bones are already in their slow water. Marrow, knuckle, oxtail. Charred ginger and onion held over an open flame until they smoke. A few stars of anise, a cinnamon stick, a whisper of clove.

Twelve hours. Sometimes more. We skim and we wait. We taste with a small spoon, add a little salt, a little patience. There are no shortcuts to a real bowl of phở — and if there were, we wouldn't take them.

— Chapter II —

Gia Đình

The Family

The recipes that opened our doors were never written down. They lived in the hands of our mother. The pinch of an aunt's fingers measuring out fish sauce. The way a grandmother knows when the broth is finished without ever looking at a clock.

We learned by watching, then by burning a few things, then by being trusted with the stockpot. That is how a recipe survives a journey across an ocean and most of a continent — by being held in someone's hands the whole way.

— Chapter III —

Castleton

Our Corner of Indy

We are tucked into a strip mall on East 82nd Street — between a kabob house and a boba shop, half a block from a grocery that smells like cardamom. The neighbors are good neighbors. The parking is honest.

Regulars tell us we are way too good for a strip mall. We say: a doorway is a doorway. The bowl is what matters.

— Chapter IV —

Hôm Nay

Today

We open at eleven. The first bowl belongs to the broth-keeper. The second goes to the regular who doesn't need to order — just nods, sits down, lets us pour. The third has a jar of house-made chili oil next to it; we made that ourselves on a slow Tuesday.

Walk in cold. Come back warm. There will be phở either way.

A bowl of phở is a quiet thing.
— House Motto · Phở Tasty
